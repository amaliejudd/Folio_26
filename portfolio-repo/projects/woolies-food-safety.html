Challenge

Food safety compliance checks — temperature logs, bench scale weight checks, hygiene checklists, sign-offs — were being completed manually and inconsistently across stores. There was no single, reliable way to confirm a check had actually happened, which created real risk around consistency and auditability.

There was also a behavioural challenge underneath the process one. Paper sheets were fast, and a lot of staff had been doing these checks the same way for years, often filling in the paperwork after finishing the physical check rather than during it, purely out of habit and muscle memory. That meant the real risk wasn’t just replacing paper with a screen, it was that any digital version could easily end up slower and clunkier than what people were already used to, and get pushed aside or worked around because of it.

Solution

A dedicated Food Safety feature within Me@Woolies let store teams complete required compliance checks digitally, on the same RF devices they already used for daily tasks, with a consistent, auditable format built in from the start.

My Design & Approach

Tobias and I went in store together early on, where the team showed us exactly how they were doing their weight scale checks by hand. Seeing it done for real made the gaps in the process obvious in a way a workshop never would have. From there I led the design end-to-end, working with Business Analysts and store team members to translate regulatory requirements into a workflow someone on the shop floor could complete quickly and correctly, without it feeling like extra admin stacked on top of their day.

The source material was the hard part. Each check arrived as a dense document — a hygiene checklist written as four blocks of prose, each with its own nested list of activities, its own result format, and store-specific exceptions written into the middle of a list. Weight check tolerances differed by department too: Bakery and Fresh Service ran one variance band with a 1kg secondary check, Customer Service and Fruit & Veg ran another with a 2kg. All of it had to come out the other side as one flow that behaved correctly wherever someone was standing.

Design Challenges

Design Challenge 1: Choosing a Build Approach Under Time Pressure

The bigger decision on this project wasn’t really about the interface, it was about how the feature should be built. I worked closely with the dev team to evaluate three technical approaches: a model-driven app, a fully custom build, and a canvas app. Model-driven was the fastest option but would have boxed in what I could actually design; fully custom gave full flexibility but wasn’t realistic within the delivery timeline. I advocated for a hybrid canvas app approach, which meant accepting some constraints on a few interactions in exchange for hitting the deadline without gutting the experience.

Design Challenge 2: Making a Check Worth Trusting

A compliance record is only as good as the conditions it was taken under, so the flow had to verify the instrument before it verified anything else. Before any weighing happens, the check asks the person to confirm the scale isn’t tagged out of order, has a green verification seal that’s present and legible, and is clean, unobstructed, balanced and showing zero. That requirement already existed on paper — what changed is that you now can’t skip past it.

The measurement steps needed to be unambiguous for someone holding a device in one hand and a calibration weight in the other. Rather than describing where a weight goes, each step shows it: a diagram of the scale with the weight drawn in position, across every configuration the checks call for. That visual approach wasn’t new — iVerify had used it before us, and when we put it in front of store SMEs they were clear they liked it, so reusing something already familiar beat inventing a new language for it.

Failing a check needed to do more than turn something red, because the consequence is physical. A failure tells the person to tag the scale “do not operate”, raise a service request and notify their Store Leader, and links straight through to the request form in Support & Feedback. We reworked that screen once already — the first version was a paragraph, the version that stuck is three numbered steps, because someone reading it is about to go and do them.

Result

Food safety compliance checks were designed to move from manual, inconsistent tracking to a single digital, auditable workflow built into the platform store teams already used every day. The feature was still in build when my engagement ended, so I handed over the completed designs along with a prioritised backlog of the remaining flows, with the gaps explicitly called out so the team knew what had been designed and what hadn’t.

What I Learnt

Choosing the canvas app over a fully custom build wasn’t the “better” technical decision on paper. Model-driven would have shipped faster and custom would have given more control. But it was the right call for what store teams actually needed in the time we had. It taught me the best build approach isn’t always the most flexible or the most polished one, it’s the one that gets a genuinely good experience into people’s hands on time.
